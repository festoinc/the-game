# Chapter 1. Laying Plans

> "Now the general who wins a battle makes many calculations in his temple ere the battle is fought. The general who loses a battle makes but few calculations beforehand. Thus do many calculations lead to victory, and few calculations to defeat: how much more no calculation at all! It is by attention to this point that I can foresee who is likely to win or lose."
>
> — Sun Tzu, *The Art of War*

Sun Tzu famously began his book with a chapter on planning. Since writing in his time was done from top to bottom, many scholars believe this placement highlights the extreme importance of the chapter.

I couldn't agree more.

When I first stepped into management, I was far from my current agreement. "Let's just take the tasks and start working on them," was my thinking. I still meet the same kind of people every day, at different levels, who want to jump immediately into action. Unfortunately, that does not lead to the project's victory.

So let's discuss Sun Tzu's original ideas about planning, along with some more modern ones that are definitely helpful.

Let's start with his famous principles:

| Sun Tzu's factor | Modern business equivalent | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Moral Law (Dao) | Culture and alignment | Shared values, trust in leadership, and commitment to common goals. |
| 2. Heaven | External conditions and uncertainty | Market trends, economic cycles, timing, and external risks beyond our control. |
| 3. Earth | Operating environment and constraints | Market structure, competition, available resources, and measurable advantages or disadvantages. |
| 4. The Commander | Leadership and management skills | The ability to make decisions, inspire confidence, exercise judgment, and take responsibility. |
| 5. Method and Discipline | Organizational processes and systems | Clear responsibilities, effective workflows, resource allocation, and consistent execution. |
