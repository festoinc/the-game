# Introduction

I've had the idea for this book for quite some time. But I think it waited for the AI era to become the perfect product-market fit. The core purpose of this book is to show that the rules of management laid out centuries ago are still relevant in today's AI world.

Even more, everyone will be able to find an example in the book that is very similar to what they have experienced in real projects.

So I present to you a book where I take ideas from Sun Tzu's *Art of War* and explain how they are still relevant in the AI era. And I'm sure they are.

The main question I want to answer in this introduction is this: Sun Tzu's book is about war, but in current project management we are mostly doing civilian projects that do not have an actual battlefield, and people are not dying.

First of all, imagine a project in air travel technology or medicine that is done badly. I think you understand where I'm going with this...

But I was able to identify the enemy we are all fighting against. It is emptiness.

Once you have the idea of a project, it exists only in your head, and you need to fight emptiness to make your project real. Through the years of my experience, I can say that emptiness is a strong opponent, and defeating it is not an easy battle.

A bright example is the Apple car. The company spent roughly 10 years and 10 billion dollars to build it, and... emptiness won. We do not have the car right now, and the project has officially closed. That is a great example of the typical battle we face when working on a new project.
